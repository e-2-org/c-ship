# container-ship: session summaries

Summaries of two Claude Code sessions on the `jekyll` branch, oldest first. The full conversations are in [transcript.md](transcript.md).

## container-ship to Jekyll: session summary

8 October 2026, working with Claude Code on the `jekyll` branch. The same session also converted e-2.org (see the `e-2` repository).

### Aim

Move container-ship (1998) to a Jekyll site on GitHub Pages, published from the e-2 organisation at e-2.org/c-ship/.

Decisions made:

- The look is modernised in the same spirit: the original lettering, buttons and drawings, in layouts that work on any screen.
- The artworks in `projects/` move to their own repositories as separate tasks, so they were left alone.
- Parts that rely on dead technology are carried over as they are and noted for later.
- CSS is plain CSS with native nesting, loaded with `@import`, as for e-2.

### What was done

**The deck** (`section.html`). The ship was a table of 29 image slices. Their positions were measured from the original in a browser and stored in `_data/deck.yml`; `_includes/deck.html` places them in percentages of the scene, so the ship scales with the window (up to 1.5 times its drawn size), with the quay still off to the right, to scroll to. Rooms and containers light up and show their pictures in the ship's two panels on hover and keyboard focus, in CSS with `:has()`, with no JavaScript. The containers are links named after the artists.

**Pages.** Generated by script from the originals (BeautifulSoup), which are in git history on `master`:

- `_rooms/`: the bridge, about c-ship, platform, communications and engine room, with the room drawings, lettering and room menu. The bridge's list of projects is in its front matter.
- `_artists/`: about each artist (`aa*.html`), with their name lettering, a picture which launches the work, the text and pictures of other work (whose captions are part of the images).
- `_works/`: each work (`ac*.html`), with its title lettering, the artist, the description, the launch picture and the credits.
- `help.html`, `intro.html`: once pop-up windows from the deck, now ordinary pages.
- `index.html`: the front page, showing the still the original used instead of its Flash intro.

Every page keeps its address. The red and orange dot buttons still reveal their labels ("index page", "about the artist", "back") on hover, and now on keyboard focus too, with alt text.

**Layouts and CSS.** `_layouts/` has `default`, `room`, `artist`, `work` and `note`. `css/c-ship.css` imports `css/modules/` (colours, base) and `css/partials/` (deck, rooms, artists, notes, front). Navy pages and white pages are set by the layout (`theme`).

### Checks

Screenshots at 1200px and 390px wide of the deck, front page, rooms, artist and work pages and help; hover tests on a container and a room; keyboard Tab order on the deck. There's no pixel comparison with the original, as the layout is deliberately new.

### Status

Staged, not committed, on `jekyll`. Run with `bundle exec jekyll serve`, then open http://localhost:4000/c-ship/.

Kept as they were, for later:

- `projects/`: the artworks, to move to their own repositories.
- The Flash intro (`index.swf`, `anim.swf`, `animdark.swf`, `cov2.swf`, `cov3.swf`), the older FutureSplash intro (`indexold.html`, `cov4.spl`) and Pig's Tails' launcher (`apgo.html`).
- `css/style.css` and `css/tabs.css`, which no page uses.

Possible next steps:

- Point the launch links at each artwork's new repository (e-2.org/<repo>/) as they move.
- The Flash intro could play with Ruffle, or be ported, as error404 and Word Perhect were.

## container-ship: session summary

14 October 2026, working with Claude Code on the `jekyll` branch, following on from the conversion on 8 October above.

### Aim

Fix errors spotted in the converted site, and carry on modernising it: more of the original lettering as text, links to the artworks' new repositories, and a page for Bird Island.

### What was done

**The deck** (`section.html`, `_includes/deck.html`, `_data/deck.yml`, `css/partials/deck.css`, `js/deck.js`):

- Room pictures are transparent GIFs, so `indexb.gif` showed through them. The panel's own image is now hidden while a room or container is lit, as the original swapped the image.
- The ship takes two thirds of the width, with the quay right-aligned in the rest at the same scale. Below 60em the quay is hidden and the ship stops shrinking (it stays the size it was at the breakpoint) and scrolls sideways.
- The deck's room and container links had the classes `room` and `container`; `.room` is also the room pages' layout, which padded the hotspots and knocked the ship out of line. They are now `deck-room` and `deck-container`.
- On a touch screen, the first tap on a room or container lights it up and shows its picture; a second tap (on it or its picture) follows the link.
- The pictures in the panels link where their room or container does. With a mouse, the last one pointed at stays lit (after resting on it for 150ms) until another is, or the pointer leaves the ship, so the picture can be reached.
- Help and the introduction open in overlays (`<dialog>`) instead of pages; `help.html` and `intro.html` still exist, sharing their text with the overlays through `_includes/help.html` and `_includes/intro.html`. The red and orange dots are now text links ("help", "intro") in the lettering font, in the colour of their dot and white on hover.
- The credit line sits at the foot of the window.

**The lettering font.** The titles, menus and names were images of a 1990s monospaced, unicase typeface that couldn't be identified. Major Mono Display was tried and didn't match, so a font was made from the images: `fonts/c-ship-lettering.woff2` ("C-Ship Lettering"). The letters in the artists' name images (13px a letter) were cut out, smoothed and traced; p, k, x, j, q, z, old-style figures and punctuation were drawn on the same grid and traced the same way. The scripts and how to rebuild it are in `_fonts/` (README there). It's used, through the `lettering` class, for:

- work titles and artist names (the bracket beside the name is CSS)
- room headings, which now show the images' wording ("programme", "ideas and intentions" ...), with the room name for screen readers
- the room menu, whose double-triangle arrows are CSS, with the filled triangle sliding over the outline one on hover, as in the original
- "back to artists and index", "index page", "about the artist" and "back", now text links with CSS dots; on the white pages they are the colour of their dot, black on hover
- the bridge's project titles

**The bridge.** Artist names are plain text links (orange, no underline) to the artist pages; project titles, without their quotes, are `h3` headings linking to the project pages, under an `h2` "Artists' projects". The front matter has `about` and `work` for each project.

**Rooms.** The menu column is as wide as the room picture (220px), so the text lines up under the heading; on small screens the header stacks under the picture with the same 1em margin as the text; room headings are 1.2em.

**Bird Island.** A project page, `_works/acjk.html`, with text from the bird-island README, Peter Moore as Technical Director, and a launch picture (`images/imljk.gif`) made from the island photo in that repo. The deck's container and the bridge link to it.

**Artworks' new homes.** The launch links on the project and artist pages point to each artwork's own repository on the e-2 site: `/adelaide/`, `/error404/`, `/tome/tome.html` (Tome has no index page), `/ahouse/`, `/nowhere/`, `/pigstails/` and `/bird-island/`. Links from the site root are no longer given the `/c-ship` prefix. Cruisin' had no link and still hasn't.

"Viewing requirements" were taken out of every project's credits.

**Clean-up.** After the work above was committed (`2c2631b`), what the branch no longer uses was removed (it's all in git history):

- 81 images not referenced by any page, layout, data file, stylesheet or script
- the Flash intro (`index.swf`, `anim.swf`, `animdark.swf`, `cov2.swf`, `cov3.swf`), the FutureSplash intro (`indexold.html`, `cov4.spl`) and Pig's Tails' launcher (`apgo.html`)
- `css/style.css` and `css/tabs.css`; the gulp setup (`gulpfile.js`, `package.json`, `package-lock.json`); the empty `.htaccess`

The 11 lettering images the font is traced from moved to `_fonts/sources/`, where `_fonts/extract.py` now reads them; a rebuild gives identical outlines. `_projects/` (the 1998 artworks, renamed from `projects/` in `2c2631b` so Jekyll doesn't publish it) is kept for reference, as each artwork is worked on in its own repository. The README was rewritten to describe the site as it now is.

### Checks

Headless Chrome, driven over the DevTools protocol: hover, touch-tap and overlay tests on the deck; measurements of the ship and quay at several widths; screenshots of each page type at desktop and phone widths, before and after the changes. After the clean-up, every local link, image, stylesheet and font in the built site (252 references) was checked: none broken.

### Status

The changes are committed in `2c2631b`; the clean-up is staged, to commit. Run with `bundle exec jekyll serve`, then open http://localhost:4000/c-ship/.

To think about:

- The lettering font is traced from a commercial typeface's rendering; fine for the site, but worth knowing before distributing the font file itself.
- Orange text on white (the "about the artist" links) has low contrast.
- Help still talks of the cursor and Netscape Navigator 3; the bridge's text for Bird Island is still the 1998 one, in the future tense; the other rooms use bold paragraphs as headings.
- Cruisin' has no repository yet; `_projects/cruisin/` (a Word document) is its only copy in git.
- The launch links only work once each artwork's repository is published under the same domain as c-ship.
- The built site has `assets/css/style.css` from GitHub Pages' default theme, which nothing uses.
